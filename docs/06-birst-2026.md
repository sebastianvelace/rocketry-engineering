# 06 · BIRST 2026 — Brazilian International Rocket Simulation Tournament

An international OpenRocket simulation competition. Entry is free, the whole
event is remote, and the entire exam is a single design challenge: build one
rocket in OpenRocket that satisfies a mission brief released only at the start
of the exam week.

- **Official site:** <https://osifog.com.br/BIRST.html>
- **Regulations:** [`BIRST_2026_Regulations_EN.pdf`](https://osifog.com.br/BIRST_2026_Regulations_EN.pdf) — consolidated, 10 pp.
- **Contact:** osifogcontato@gmail.com · Instagram [@birst_osifog](https://www.instagram.com/birst_osifog) · YouTube [@osifog.oficial](https://www.youtube.com/@osifog.oficial)

## One competition, two names

The event is administratively a single thing under two designations
(Regulations §8.1–8.2):

| Designation | Used for |
|---|---|
| **OSIFOG** — *Olimpíada Brasileira de Simulação de Foguetes* | Brazilian audiences, official documents |
| **BIRST** — Brazilian International Rocket Simulation Tournament | International outreach |

Any reference to one is legally equivalent to the other. Expect both in
communications; do not treat them as separate events.

## Eligibility

Three levels by educational stage (§2.1):

1. Elementary School
2. High School
3. **Open Adult / Undergraduate**

**International participants may register for Level 3 only** (§2.3.3), at any age
and educational level (§2.3.9). National and international entrants compete in a
single unified Level 3 ranking (§2.3.2, §6.2.3).

Teams are **1 to 3 people** (§3.3.1). Mixed national/international teams are
allowed (§3.3.6). Registration is individual; teams form later (§3.1.2).

Participation is free at every phase (§2.2.4).

## How it actually runs

1. **Register individually** through the international form and join a Google
   Classroom class (§2.2.2, §3.2.1). Classroom is the official platform for
   announcements, tutorials and the exam.
2. **Submit the Team Registration Form** — a *separate* form from step 1 (§3.3.3).
   It fixes the team name and designates the captain (§3.3.4).
3. **Secret Mission released** at the start of the exam window. It contains the
   mission brief, the numeric constraints, the scoring formula, and the
   definition of the critical elements (§4.1.1, §5.2.1, §5.3.2).
4. **Seven days** to build the project in OpenRocket (§4.1.2).
5. **The captain submits the `.ork`** through the official submission form
   (§3.3.5, §4.4.1).

There are no theory questions of any kind — no multiple choice, no essays. It is
one practical design challenge (§4.3.3).

### Required software

**the Classroom material is not optional reading**, it is the only documentation
for two of the three mandatory tools.

## The sharp edges

Everything below can end the run regardless of how good the design is.

**Team name and file name.** The Team Registration Form requires a name built as
`NAME + SURNAME + 4-digit number` (example given: `Olimpianos Crew 3140`). The
form states that the submitted `.ork` filename must **exactly match** the
registered team name, or the project is **disqualified**.

> Note a wording conflict worth resolving before submitting: the form says the
> filename must match the team name exactly, while §4.4.1 says to name the file
> with "the corresponding level **and** the official team name." Those two
> instructions are not the same string. Ask the organizers, and keep the reply.

**Critical elements.** An error in any element flagged critical is an **automatic**
disqualification (§5.3.1). The critical elements are defined only inside the
Secret Mission — so the first task of exam week is to enumerate them and build a
checklist before touching the design.

**No notification.** Disqualified teams are not told (§5.3.3). They simply do not
appear in the ranking, and the participation certificate is invalidated. There is
an appeal window after results, by email only (§5.3.4).

**Form discipline.** International entrants must not use national forms, and vice
versa (§2.2.3, §2.3.4). The form's title carries full authority on which is which.

**Ordering.** The Team Registration Form must be completed **before** the Secret
Mission is released, or the team cannot sit the exam (§3.3.2).

**Deliverable format.** `.ork` only. Screenshots and images may also be requested
(§4.4.2). Videos are never requested (§4.4.3). Physical rockets are not accepted —
the event is purely digital (§4.4.4).

## Evaluation

Scoring is **strictly mathematical**, computed from values read out of the
OpenRocket file. There is no subjective, aesthetic or artistic component
(§5.1.1).

Named metrics (§5.1.2):

- apogee
- **stability coefficient**
- **angle of attack**
- instantaneous thrust
- stage landing location
- others defined per mission

The scoring expression is released with the Secret Mission and applied identically
to every team at a level (§5.2.1–5.2.2). The regulations give an illustrative form
only:

```
Score = 10 − (3 − T)² + M/300,  if M ≥ 300   (qualified)
Disqualification                if M < 300
```

That shape is worth reading closely even though the real formula differs: it
combines a **squared penalty for missing a target** with a **linear reward for a
maximised quantity**, plus a **hard constraint whose violation disqualifies**.
Optimising the reward term while drifting off the target is how you lose.

### Awards

Certificates are distributed by percentile, independently per level (§6.2.1):

| Certificate | Share of teams |
|---|---|
| Gold | top **6 %** |
| Silver | next **12 %** |
| Bronze | next **18 %** |
| Honourable Mention | next **14 %** |
| Participation | remaining **50 %** |

Half of all teams receive a graded award; everyone receives some certificate
(§6.2.2). Non-awarded scores may not be published (§6.1.2).

## What is explicitly permitted

Unusually permissive, and stated outright (§7.2.3–7.2.4):

- **AI use is permitted.** OSIFOG explicitly recognises it as an educational tool.
- Bibliographic research, external references, and consulting other people are all
  allowed.
- Plagiarism is the line, and it is an immediate disqualification (§7.2.2).

Submitting a project also grants the organisation the right to use the team name,
renders and screenshots promotionally (§7.2.1).

## Why this project has an edge here

The competition is scored on numbers that OpenRocket computes — specifically
**stability coefficient** and **angle of attack**. This repository's
[open-source track](05-open-source.md) is an upstream contribution to exactly
that part of OpenRocket's simulation core.

Two findings from that work bear directly on a competition submission:

**1. A stable rocket could abort spuriously.** Upstream issue
[#3183](https://github.com/openrocket/openrocket/issues/3183): a statically stable
rocket in a crosswind could emit `TUMBLE_UNDER_THRUST` and abort roughly a metre
off the pad, because the tumble test compared CG against a CP produced by an
aerodynamic model already outside its validity envelope. If a mission brief
involves wind, a sound design can produce a catastrophic-looking simulation
through no fault of the design.

**2. Simulations with wind turbulence were not reproducible.** Upstream issue
[#3188](https://github.com/openrocket/openrocket/issues/3188): the simulation's
random seed never reached the wind models. Two runs of the same configuration
could differ — measured on the bundled Alpha III, from 1.05 m to 131 m of apogee
with an identical seed.

**The practical consequence for BIRST is the same in both cases: the number the
judges read out of the submitted `.ork` may not be the number seen when it was
built.** Evaluation is a detailed reading of each project (§5.1.3), performed on
the judges' machines.

Two defences, both cheap:

- **If the mission permits it, submit with turbulence intensity at zero.** A
  deterministic wind field removes the variance entirely. Confirm the brief allows
  it before relying on this.
- **Otherwise, re-run the final file several times and record the spread**, then
  design with margin against that spread rather than against a single run. A
  target hit only on the median run is a target missed half the time.
